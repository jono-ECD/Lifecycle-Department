# Brand voice — Blessed Botanicals

_Captured 2026-09-21 from the client Copy Guide (Drive
`1nDDU2k3yqksGm3r9PrubllxtcHyPPmAPJjJD3T6T_lo`, updated 2026-09-21) and the ECD
NTK (May 2026). Re-confirm if either source changes._

How this brand sounds. Specific enough that two writers produce recognisably the
same voice.

## Voice in one line

Warm, grounded and specific — someone who understands why you're looking for
another way, and answers with the herb, not the adjective.

## Sounds like

- **Hopeful** — empowering rather than fear-based. The reader is not behind.
- **Grounded** — every emotional opening lands on something concrete: what the
  product supports, what's in it, where it fits in a day.
- **Faith-rooted, lightly** — "Blessed" reads as gratitude, care and purpose.
  Never a sermon.

## Does not sound like

More useful than the positive description:

- **Clinical or cold.** This is a family brand built from a personal story.
- **Vague wellness-speak.** "Feel your best", "transform your health",
  "wellness journey" used *instead of* explaining the product.
- **Shaming.** Never make the customer feel behind or wrong for what they tried
  before.
- **Overwhelming.** Routines must feel doable. Jargon stacks are a failure.
- **Gimmicky.** Spinning wheels, countdown-FOMO blasts.
- **AI-smooth and hollow.** The Copy Guide calls this out by name and the NTK
  warns the client will catch it.

## The emotional promise

> **It's time to take your health back.**

Customers usually arrive after frustration — synthetic ingredients, long labels,
harsh formulas, products that promised a lot and did little. The brand's answer
is a simpler path: whole herbs, clean formulas, a routine that fits a real day.

## The founder story

Brittany was in a pre-med program when she met Ashton, who had been diagnosed
with late-stage Lyme disease. When conventional options fell short, she began
researching herbal approaches to support him. That research became the
small-batch formulas the brand now sells.

The NTK calls this **the #1 conversion driver — use it everywhere.** Customers
managing chronic conditions see themselves in it.

> Never state or imply that herbal formulas treated, cured or resolved Ashton's
> Lyme disease. Tell the story as lived experience, not as evidence of efficacy.
> See [`messaging-constraints.md`](messaging-constraints.md).

## Structure that works

The Copy Guide's closing line is the template:

> **Name the need. Explain the herb. Show the routine. Keep the promise grounded.**

Start with the customer's frustration or desire, then move quickly to the
simpler answer. Don't stay emotional too long.

The reader should finish thinking: *This makes sense. I understand what this
does. I can build a routine around this. This feels cleaner than what I've
tried.*

## Specificity rule

The single most important mechanic in this voice. Broad wellness language may
support the message but can never replace the specifics.

| Not enough | Instead |
|---|---|
| "Calm nights" | Calms the nervous system; racing thoughts; gentle evening routine without heavy sedation |
| "Time to detox" | Liver Cleanse is foundational bile/liver support; Candida Cleanse is targeted microbial balance — explain each role |
| "Daily wellness" | Energy, nutrient intake, antioxidants, amino acids, vitamins, and why the leaf matters |
| "Clean sun care" | Non-nano zinc oxide, hyaluronic acid, botanical oils, lightweight daily wear |
| "Organic Moringa" | Organic moringa *leaves* — the nutrient-dense part — extracted in small batches in vegetable glycerin and distilled water |

Ingredient detail always carries a *why it matters* clause.

It's time to take your health back. · Build your wellness routine. ·
Consistency changes everything. · Simple, clean formulas. · Whole herbs. ·
Intentional blends. · No fillers. · No synthetic additives. · Nothing
unnecessary. · Made with care. · Rooted in purpose. · There's no one-size-fits-all

A simpler way to support your routine. · Clean ingredients. Small-batch formulas.
Real herbs your body can recognize. · Wellness starts somewhere. · It starts with
a decision. · The small choices add up. · Take the next step. · Feeling Blessed.

**"Feeling Blessed"** is the recurring review/social-proof section name — it ties
proof to the brand without forcing it.

## Purity positioning

Lead with what is **not** in the product. Zero synthetic fillers, zero synthetic
fragrance, bioavailable plant ingredients only. The NTK notes this overcomes
scepticism about supplement-industry quality, which is a primary objection.

## AI use

The Copy Guide is explicit: AI may be used for early ideation or rough drafting,
but **final copy must always be reviewed, fact-checked and refined by a human
writer.** AI output is never published as-is. This is a standing requirement on
every skill that touches this account's copy.

## The gut check

Before approving copy:

- [ ] Does it name a specific wellness need?
- [ ] Does it explain how the product fits a routine?
- [ ] Does it avoid disease claims and overpromising?
- [ ] Does it feel simple, clean and intentional?
- [ ] Is it warm without becoming vague?
- [ ] **Could this apply to any wellness brand?** If yes, make it more specific.
