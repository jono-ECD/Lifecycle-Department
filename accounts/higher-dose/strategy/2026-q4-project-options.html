1

BFCM abandonment system by product line

Madison's top priority and the largest flow revenue at stake.

Tier 1Live by Nov 6Effort M
Strategy

Rebuild the BFCM abandonment layer, which drove most of last year's abandonment revenue, then make it product-line specific. The sauna blanket browse test is the proof point.

Tactics
  • BFCM versions of browse, cart and checkout: the code, the gift timer, the shipping cutoff
  • Branch content by the product line viewed (recovery, beauty, hair growth)
  • Shorter delays: cart 2h → 30 min, browse 4h → 1–2h
  • SMS first in checkout abandonment
  • Forms in flows: an onsite reminder form after email 2
  • A clicked-but-didn't-buy branch offering the gift, not a deeper discount
Evidence

Abandonment flows earned $1.18M in Q4 2025, 45% of all flow revenue. $741K of that came from BFCM versions that are now archived.

Sizing≈ $118K per +10%

Matching last year's abandonment revenue (about $1.18M) is the baseline. Every 10% lift on that adds about $118K. The sauna blanket test sets the lift.

Measure

Holdout group per flow, revenue per recipient by product line, recovery rate

Depends on

Current Project 2 scope, sauna blanket test result, whether Alia and Klaviyo forms can run together

Risk

The Instant Audiences flows are in draft and September revenue dropped. Find out why before building.

2

Loyalty on Rediem: flows and balance activation

Ingrid's "answer to October sales without another sale."

Tier 1Start nowEffort S
Strategy

Turn the points they already owe customers into revenue, with no new discount. Rebuild the loyalty flows that disappeared in the Rediem migration, then activate balances in controlled batches.

Tactics
  • Rebuild the loyalty welcome, reward-available and expiry-warning flows on Rediem events
  • A $10–50 balance segment, released in 500-profile batches as Ingrid asked
  • A dynamic balance block in campaigns and abandonment emails
  • After the first holiday purchase: "use your balance on order two"
Evidence

LoyaltyLion flows earned $146K in Q4 2025, including $84K from the loyalty welcome in December alone. No loyalty flows are live today. Rediem's points-earned, expiry and redemption events are already in Klaviyo.

Sizing≈ $100–150K in Q4

Assumes the rebuilt flow set performs like last year's. The batch test then sets the redemption rate for balance activation.

Measure

Segment revenue vs. a holdout, redemptions per batch, points liability drawdown for finance

Depends on

Whether Rediem events carry the points balance as a property

Risk

Legal sign-off on promoting loyalty, and finance's comfort with the roughly 30M outstanding points

3

Second-purchase engine: predictive and Personalization Phase 2

The main CLTV lever: turn first orders into second orders.

Tier 2BFCM slice + Q1Effort M–L
Strategy

Make the first 75 days after a purchase a journey for each entry product. Extend the one-email personalization flows into sequences built on Klaviyo's predictive data.

Tactics
  • Extend the RFM flows to 3–4 touches: education, proof, next product, then an offer only if needed
  • Expected-date-of-next-order timing test (−14, −7 or 0 days) with a product-line feed
  • Second-product paths by entry product (mat, blanket, red light, hat)
  • A bundle variant during BFCM, using the new Next Best Product cross-sell flow (launched Sep 29)
Evidence

Only 21.4% of customers reorder (median 75 days). 45,844 first-time buyers from the past year haven't. Customers whose first order was a mat reorder at about 25% (approximate cohort).

Sizing≈ $263K per +1 pt

Each point of second-order rate among the 45,844 is about 458 orders. At the 2026 AOV of $573, that's about $263K over 12 months.

Measure

Second-order rate, days to second order, 90-day CLTV by group, holdouts

Depends on

Project 5 product-line properties, next-best-product mapping

Framing

Present it as Phase 2 of the personalization work, the natural next step after a one-email build.

5

Customer intelligence foundation

Turn Ingrid's personas and product lines into data Klaviyo can act on.

Tier 1 thin · Tier 2 fullEffort M
Strategy

Agree one taxonomy, write it to every profile and use it everywhere. Branch messaging by benefit and choose products by technology.

Tactics
  • Get the client's decision on the benefit × technology taxonomy
  • Profile properties: primary_benefit, tech_owned, entry_product, persona, rfm_tier, loyalty_band
  • Catalog insights for each entry product: who buys, what they buy next, how long until they reorder
  • A customer journey map built from the personas deck plus Shopify and Klaviyo data through Claude
Evidence

Ingrid described two ways to classify products: by benefit (recovery, beauty, hair growth, longevity) and by technology (PEMF, infrared, red light, microcurrent). Mau's deck lists predictive segments already built, for example Expected Purchase 30 Days (2,999).

SizingEnabler

This doesn't earn revenue directly. Its value shows up in Projects 1–4. Track the share of profiles classified.

Measure

Share of profiles with a primary benefit, number of segments ready for testing

Governance

Bulk writes of profile properties and tags change a live account. They need the client owner's approval for each change. Prefer flow "update property" steps or a CSV import, and test on a sample first.

Method and data notes
  • Source: HigherDOSE Klaviyo account, queried read-only on Oct 5, 2026. Revenue is Placed Order value under the account's attribution settings, by event date.
  • The Klaviyo Q4 2025 total ($18.5M) is gross order value. Mau's deck uses Shopify net sales ($16.57M). Don't mix the two in one chart.
  • Flow revenue was grouped by flow name, including archived 2025 flows (BFCM versions, LoyaltyLion). Revenue on SMS reached Klaviyo only from June 2026.
  • Repeat-purchase figures come from Klaviyo's analytics query over about three years of orders. The mat cohort matches product titles containing "mat", so treat it as approximate.
  • 2026 AOV of $573 is Jan–Sep Klaviyo Placed Order value divided by order count.
  • Sizing figures are scenarios on last year's baselines, not forecasts.
